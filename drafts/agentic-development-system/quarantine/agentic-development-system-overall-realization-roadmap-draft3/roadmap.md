## Workstreams

A workstream is a planning and evidence grouping. It is not automatically a task, worker assignment, approval, or implementation authorization.

### A. Design governance and current-target representation

Realize the minimum durable representation for target-design identity, accepted-envelope linkage, current-versus-target distinction, component anchors, literal-link discovery, revision/supersession, unresolved-question classification, human-facing views, and migration relationships. Preserve current `as-is.md` authority until a reviewed compatibility path exists.

**Entry:** Draft-11 acceptance and the affected current anchors are available.

**Exit:** A reviewed bounded detail plan and implementation packet identify exact records, owners, consumers, protected inputs, compatibility behavior, validation, recovery, and the conditions requiring renewed Human Review. A representative artifact is traceable to each affected anchor.

**Stop:** Missing/contradictory anchor, unbounded consumer set, silent current-record rewrite, or material change to goal, boundary, authority, acceptance, risk, protected input, or external effect.

### B. Lifecycle, orchestration, and authority

Realize the three-phase lifecycle and distinguish Interactive Design / Prototyping, Human Review, detail planning, kick-off, task preparation, task-start handoff, task-control admission, implementation, validation, candidate proof, benchmark protocol approval, advancement, target adoption, and merge. Implement progressive-disclosure status and feedback routing without making the front-face router an implicit implementation authority.

**Entry:** Workstream A provides identity and relationship facts; current routing and task-control behavior remain baseline.

**Exit:** A bounded flow records each transition, decision holder, exact revision, next action, and authority limit, and fails closed when authority or required evidence is missing.

**Stop:** Review is confused with approval, feedback silently changes active work, decision holder is absent, or a blocking question lacks an owner and safe checkpoint.

### C. Accepted agent roster and skill composition realization

Implement the accepted draft-11 target direction without reopening it as a design question: adapt the `as-is` front face, retain/adapt `component-builder` and `evidence-validator`, retain `execution-advisor` and compose `expert`, deprecate/replace `thinking-companion` only through consumer-backed migration, defer the `worker` replacement decision as recorded, introduce the design/prototyping role, and preserve the accepted skill dispositions and proposed master compositions. Skills remain procedures and never grant authority or tools.

**Entry:** Workstreams A–B establish target identity, authority, and lifecycle; current consumer inventory is available before replacement or retirement.

**Exit:** Each changed role/skill has a source-to-target mapping, owner, consumers, compatibility path, capability profile, focused positive/negative validation, recovery path, and explicit migration/retirement gate. At least one target composition is exercised while legacy paths remain available where needed.

**Stop:** Roster design is silently reopened, a model name becomes an architectural role, a skill gains hidden authority, an unavailable capability is silently substituted, or a source consumer lacks a migration path.

**Historical caution:** Draft-36’s detailed roster and ledger can inform this workstream, but they are not silently substituted for the accepted draft-11 dispositions. Contradictions are recorded and escalated.

### D. Implementation packets, plan injection, and assurance

**Entry:** Workstreams A–C supply accepted identity, authority, role, and capability facts for the bounded unit.

**Exit:** A packet can be frozen, admitted, executed, validated, and handed off with attributable evidence; missing or contradictory inputs stop affected work.

**Stop:** Packet ambiguity changes scope or acceptance, protected controls are not adequate to the risk, required checks are unavailable, or parent and child evidence cannot be separated.

### F. Host, setup, consumption, and isolation

Prove repository-local setup and a separately owned mock consumer with candidate and pinned-master copies in controlled distinct locations. Preserve configuration and project-local state. State precisely what is prompt-guided, audited, or enforced; do not claim strong filesystem, network, credential, process, untrusted-project, installation, portability, upgrade, downgrade, uninstall, or multi-project isolation without separate evidence.

**Entry:** Target capability needs are known and candidate behavior exists; the seed and protected setup controls are separately owned.

**Exit:** Setup-inclusive local proof passes without unrelated configuration changes or state leakage, with explicit limitations.

**Stop:** Setup overwrites unrelated state, candidate/baseline state leaks, unexpected credentials/external effects occur, or weak controls are presented as enforcement.

### G. Migration, compatibility, and recovery

Inventory current consumers, map source artifacts to accepted target compositions, retain legacy paths, migrate one bounded consumer or component at a time, preserve recovery, and retire only after replacement parity and consumer closure. Treat post-benchmark migration as a separate activity from pre-benchmark inventory and compatibility planning.

**Entry:** Candidate proof has produced reviewed evidence and the proposed migration scope has named owners and consumers.

**Exit:** Every migrated item has before/after evidence, compatibility behavior, recovery option, owner, and removal gate; the evaluated candidate revision and merge target remain traceable.

**Stop:** Consumer inventory is incomplete, behavior diverges without a decision, recovery is unavailable, or the merge target differs from the evaluated revision without revalidation.

### H. Setup-inclusive benchmark and advancement

Freeze the common seed, pinned `master` baseline revision, candidate revision, feature, setup, model/configuration, budget, retry policy, deterministic checks, protected validators/scorer, rubric, safety-failure policy, thresholds, advancement rule, and report format. Run equivalent baseline and candidate cases, review results, and decide advancement only for bounded claims.

**Entry:** Candidate proof and setup evidence exist; benchmark protocol is separately reviewed and approved.

**Exit:** Reproducible paired results, safety/failure accounting, bounded claims, and an explicit advancement decision exist.

**Stop:** Candidate behavior is absent, conditions are inequivalent, controls are mutable by the candidate, safety/authority failure occurs, or thresholds are changed after observing results.

### I. Target adoption and merge

After advancement, decide separately whether to adopt target contracts/current-record changes, retire or retain source artifacts, and merge a precise scope into `master`. Revalidate against the actual merge target after evaluation. A bounded compatible slice may merge earlier only by explicit exception and must not be described as full-target adoption.

**Entry:** Advancement evidence, compatibility/migration closure for the exact scope, clean scoped candidate state, and recovery path.

**Exit:** Explicit human adoption and merge decisions, current-target validation, focused/regression/setup evidence on the actual merge target, and preserved recovery/reversal.

**Stop:** Any protected or unrelated change is present, source consumers are unaccounted for, target drift invalidates evidence, or approval is inferred from benchmark rank or green tests.

## Dependency and concurrency model

Planning of independent workstreams may proceed in parallel when each has stable inputs and a separate owner. Implementation and evidence transitions are ordered by their dependencies. In particular:

- A–D provide identity, authority, role, packet, and assurance facts needed by the selected E slice, but unrelated portions of A–D need not become a blanket prerequisite for all implementation.
- E is the first execution-control implementation slice and may proceed after its own bounded design, ownership, and admission prerequisites are closed.
- F depends on actual candidate behavior and cannot be proven from plans or baseline fixture tests.
- G may prepare inventory and compatibility plans before H, but migration execution and artifact retirement follow evidence and advancement rather than precede them.
- H compares a candidate against a pinned `master` baseline only after candidate and setup proof exist.
- I follows benchmark review and is separate from advancement, target adoption, retirement, and merge.

This model prevents both errors: treating draft 6 as the whole program, and forcing every future workstream to block the first bounded pilot without evidence that it is a dependency.

## Pilot versus program closure

### Pilot-scope closure

Pilot closure is available when the selected candidate slice has implemented and passed its own focused structures, candidate fixture and setup proof, baseline regression, evidence review, residual-risk disposition, and—if benchmarked—the approved paired evaluation and advancement decision. Pilot closure supports only claims about the tested slice and its conditions.

### Program-scope closure

Program closure requires evidence that the accepted broad target has either been realized or explicitly deferred by authorized decisions. This includes the lifecycle and current-target representation, accepted agent and skill direction, bounded packet and authority controls, parent/child realization, assurance, host/setup commitments made by the program, migration/compatibility obligations, and the evaluation/adoption decisions. It does not require implementing explicitly deferred future workloads or unsupported distribution/isolation claims, but those deferrals must remain recorded and not be presented as complete.

## Benchmark and merge answer

### Earliest defensible benchmark

The branch becomes benchmarkable for a **pilot-scope workflow comparison** only after:

1. the exact bounded candidate slice is selected and its required design/detail plans are reviewed;
2. the candidate workflow and required structures are implemented;
3. focused candidate checks and relevant baseline regressions pass;
4. the repository-local candidate proof and setup path exist;
5. the mock consumer, common seed, protected validator/scorer, and feature are frozen;
6. the candidate revision and pinned `master` baseline revision are identified;
7. equivalent settings, budgets, retries, deterministic checks, and failure policy are frozen; and
8. the user separately approves the exact benchmark protocol and advancement rule.

The result is a pilot comparison, not full-program evidence.

### Earliest defensible merge with `master`

A merge is justified only after:

1. the bounded candidate scope has passed its approved evaluation or an explicitly approved compatible-slice exception applies;
2. evidence review supports the claims being made, with no safety-critical or authority failure;
3. source consumers and affected records have a bounded migration/compatibility disposition;
4. current behavior remains available where migration is incomplete;
5. target adoption/current-record update is explicitly approved for the exact scope, if required;
6. the candidate is revalidated against the actual merge-target `master` revision after evaluation;
7. focused, regression, setup, and candidate checks pass on that merge target;
8. protected and unrelated files are unchanged or explicitly accounted for;
9. recovery/reversal remains available; and
10. the human or authorized integrator separately authorizes the exact merge.

The branch need not contain the entire long-term target before a bounded compatible slice can merge. However, that merge must be described as a bounded advancement, not as completion of the full rearchitecture. A full-program merge claim requires program-scope closure evidence.
